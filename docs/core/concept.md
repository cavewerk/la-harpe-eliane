# Concept

## What it is

La harpe Éliane is an instrument: eight physical strings, each set into
vibration by its own electromagnet driven by additive synthesis, played live
through hardware controllers. It is an **ongoing design project**, not a
finished object — composing with it and designing it are one continuous
process. ([C1](claims.md#c1))

## The string in place of the speaker

Topologically, la harpe Éliane resembles a conventional electronic
instrument: a transducer renders an otherwise inaudible signal (the
computational material) audible. Usually that transducer is a speaker; here
the speaker is replaced by the string — a profoundly alive material, and one
the author has a life-long, intimate connection with as both performer and
technician. What emerges from this coupling (timbres inaccessible through
traditional means, [C6](claims.md#c6)) is the primary site of inquiry.
(Hexagram summary, 2026-10-05)

## A family of instruments

La harpe Éliane may become a family of instruments in different sizes, as the
violin family does. Members are **not named yet**: so far all instruments are
roughly the same size, and *la harpe Éliane* is used for all of them.

Currently: **a modular array of eight strings in two four-string modules** of
about the same size. Describe it as one instrument — a modular array that can
be distributed throughout a space — not as "two instruments". (2026-10-04)

## Not automation

The instrument is computational, but its aim is not automation. It depends on
the performer's continuous presence and attention; it does not play itself.
([C7](claims.md#c7))

## Lineage

The work of Éliane Radigue is the primary influence — on the sound the author
wants to hear, on composition, and on design decisions themselves. Other
sources contribute, but Radigue is the centre. ([C1](claims.md#c1))

## Feed-forward, not feedback

Feedback is a common, often central, technique in resonating-string
instruments, and it was part of Radigue's own practice (microphone feedback,
oscillator feedback). La harpe Éliane deliberately uses a **feed-forward**
chain instead: oscillator → electromagnet → string, with no signal returning
from the string to the electronics. ([C2](claims.md#c2))

## Instability without a loop

Removing feedback does not make the instrument predictable. In practice it is
arguably *more* unstable: a tuning is set, a magnet driven, the string
resonates for a while — and then may seize up. Possible causes include
hysteresis and residual motion from previous excitation (the string had not
fully stopped). ([C3](claims.md#c3))

## Fragility as performance stance

The author embraces this behaviour — not instability for its own sake, but a
**fragility** that demands **constant attention** while also demanding that
the performer **not do too much**. Presence and restraint at once.
([C4](claims.md#c4))
